<p class="section-lede" style="max-width:70ch;">Probability is the one language every other subject on this site already speaks: the <a href="{{ '/vision/nonlinear-optimization/slam/' | relative_url }}">SLAM</a> and <a href="{{ '/vision/multi-view-geometry/ransac/' | relative_url }}">RANSAC</a> parts of the vision guides sample and score hypotheses, the <a href="{{ 'https://denimpatel.github.io/AI/robotics/navigation/odometry/' | relative_url }}">navigation notes</a> fuse noisy measurements, and the <a href="{{ '/ai/llm-training/language-models/' | relative_url }}">language-model guides</a> are cross-entropy all the way down. This volume builds that language from the ground up. It starts at what a probability actually is and ends at the limit theorems that let a sample stand in for a distribution &mdash; the two results every estimator rests on.</p>

<p class="section-lede" style="max-width:70ch;">The companion volume, <a href="{{ '/math/probability-in-action/' | relative_url }}">Probability in Action</a>, takes these foundations and runs them: maximum likelihood, Bayesian inference, estimation theory, Monte Carlo, Markov chains, MCMC, and the filters and models the rest of the site is built on. The density change-of-variables you may have met in <a href="{{ '/math/calculus-in-motion/probability/' | relative_url }}">Calculus in Motion</a> is the calculus of this subject; here we do the probability of it, and the two are written to be read side by side. Keep the <a href="{{ '/math/probability/glossary/' | relative_url }}">glossary</a> open for notation.</p>
