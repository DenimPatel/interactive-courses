<h3 class="era-heading">Why this section exists</h3>
<p class="section-lede" style="max-width:68ch;">Every other subject here leans on linear algebra, calculus, probability and statistics it never teaches. The <a href="{{ '/vision/multi-view-geometry/' | relative_url }}">multi-view geometry</a> guide factorises an essential matrix and reasons about RANSAC inlier probabilities, the <a href="{{ '/vision/nonlinear-optimization/' | relative_url }}">nonlinear optimization</a> guide solves normal equations and takes Newton steps, and the <a href="{{ '/ai/llm-training/' | relative_url }}">LLM guides</a> are matrix multiplies, gradients, cross-entropy and calibration all the way down. This section is where that machinery gets built properly &mdash; geometry first, algebra second, every idea attached to something you can drag.</p>
<p class="section-lede" style="max-width:68ch;"><strong>Linear Algebra, Interactively</strong> starts at what a vector is and ends at attention matrices, Gauss-Newton and SO(3). <strong>Calculus, Interactively</strong> is single-variable calculus first, carried by a robot moving along a line, then multivariable calculus, carried by a two-link arm whose Jacobian <em>is</em> the derivative as a matrix; <strong>Calculus in Motion</strong> lets that arm move &mdash; vector fields, constrained optimization, matrix calculus and backprop, differential equations, and calculus on manifolds. Neither calculus volume re-teaches gradient descent, Newton's method or the matrix exponential as algorithms &mdash; those live in Nonlinear Optimization, which these parts feed by name. Every part in every series ends with a "where this shows up" line pointing at the page elsewhere on the site that uses it.</p>
<p class="section-lede" style="max-width:68ch;"><strong>Probability, Interactively</strong> begins at what a probability means and works up through counting, conditioning, Bayes' rule, random variables and the limit theorems. <strong>Probability in Action</strong> takes those foundations and runs them: likelihood and Bayesian inference, estimators and intervals, sampling and Monte Carlo, Markov chains and MCMC, information theory, and finally the Bayes, Kalman and particle filters that <a href="https://denimpatel.github.io/AI/robotics/navigation/odometry/">robot navigation</a> and <a href="{{ '/vision/nonlinear-optimization/slam/' | relative_url }}">SLAM</a> are built from, plus the softmax, cross-entropy and calibration the <a href="{{ '/ai/llm-training/language-models/' | relative_url }}">language-model guides</a> assume. The change-of-variables calculus those models need is in <a href="{{ '/math/calculus-in-motion/probability/' | relative_url }}">Calculus in Motion</a>; here we do the probability of it. <strong>Statistics, Interactively</strong> is the newest volume: 32 parts from a single sample you can redraw through estimation, testing and Bayesian inference to the Kalman and particle filters the robotics guides run, ending at causal inference. It assumes <a href="{{ '/math/probability/' | relative_url }}">probability</a> as a prerequisite &mdash; sample spaces, conditional probability, random variables, expectation and the named distributions, plus the statements of the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem &mdash; and Part 1 recaps only the load-bearing pieces.</p>
