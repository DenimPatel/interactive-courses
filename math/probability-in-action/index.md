<p class="section-lede" style="max-width:70ch;">Volume I built the rules. This volume does the work. Given data and a model, how do you estimate the parameters &mdash; and how do you know whether your estimator is any good? How do you draw samples when the distribution has no closed form, and how do you average over a space too large to enumerate? How much information does an observation carry, and what does a model that assigns probabilities to sentences actually believe? Each answer is an algorithm, and each algorithm has a canvas.</p>

<p class="section-lede" style="max-width:70ch;">The last act is where the site cashes in: the <a href="{{ '/ai/llm-training/language-models/' | relative_url }}">language-model</a> guides consume cross-entropy and the softmax, <a href="{{ '/vision/multi-view-geometry/ransac/' | relative_url }}">multi-view geometry</a> consumes RANSAC and robust losses, and robot navigation consumes the Bayes filter in all three of its guises &mdash; histograms, Gaussians and particles. The <a href="{{ '/math/probability/' | relative_url }}">first volume</a> is the prerequisite; nothing here needs more than it taught. A <a href="{{ '/math/probability-in-action/glossary/' | relative_url }}">routing table</a> maps every idea to the page that uses it.</p>
